FormFill · end-to-end workflow

What happens between uploading a form and finishing it

A walkthrough of the full experience, page by page, for anyone who hasn't read the code.

01

The five pages of a session

A person using FormFill moves through five screens, in order, to go from "I have a form" to a downloaded, completed document.

1 · Upload Choose or drag in a PDF, PNG, or JPG.
2 · Review See what questions were found on the form.
3 · Overview See the form's sections and how long it'll take.
4 · Answer Answer one question at a time.
5 · Confirm & download Hear every answer read back, then download the finished document.
02

What happens behind the "Upload" step

The moment someone continues past the upload page, the whole PDF is sent to an AI model in a single call, which reads it the way a person would: the AI is shown the actual document, not just text pulled out of it. A quick, free, local check first lists any real clickable fields the PDF already has (the kind you could type straight into in Adobe Acrobat), so the model can match its own questions back onto them where they exist. Photos and non-PDF images aren't supported yet; FormFill tells the person clearly rather than failing silently or guessing.

03

Turning a form into real questions

The AI model is asked to do one job: group the form into sections and turn each part into a clear question a person could answer, whether or not the PDF has real fillable fields underneath.

For example, the model turns the officialese "Applicant's declaration of relevant prior interests" into the plain question "Have you had a financial interest in this before?", while keeping the original wording available too. It's never hidden.

What's sent to the AI: the whole PDF file itself, not just extracted text. This is a deliberate tradeoff (an earlier, text-only approach turned out to be unreliable across real forms) with a real privacy cost: information rendered inside the PDF can't be automatically hidden from the model the way plain text could be. See the known-risks doc. Whatever the person later types as an answer is never sent anywhere.

See the model & pricing doc for which AI model does this, what it costs, and how it was tested.

04

Review, Overview, Answer, Confirm

PageWhat the person seesStatus
ReviewEvery detected question, each tagged as high, medium, or low confidence. Low-confidence ones are flagged for a closer look, never guessed silently.working
OverviewThe form broken into its sections, with an estimate of how many minutes it'll take.working
AnswerOne question at a time, in plain language, with keyboard shortcuts to move forward, back, repeat, or get help.working
ConfirmEvery answer read back before the session can be marked complete. This step cannot be skipped. Confirming downloads a real document.working

Answers are only ever stored in the person's own browser for that session. They're never sent to a server and never saved permanently unless the person chooses to.

05

What downloading actually produces

Forms with real fillable fields: a genuinely filled copy of the original PDF, with every answer written into its real field and the file flattened so it looks and prints correctly.
Flat forms (the common case): a plain summary document listing every question and answer. This isn't a marked-up copy of the original form, since there's no reliable way yet to know exactly where on the page each answer should be drawn.
Only PDF uploads are handled. A scanned or image-only PDF is still read by the AI model the same way as any other PDF. A photo or non-PDF image file isn't supported yet.

See the known-risks doc for a fuller list of current limitations and what could go wrong in a live demo.
