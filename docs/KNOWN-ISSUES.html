FormFill · known risks

Things that can break, and what to do about them

A plain list of real risks found while building and testing. These are things that actually happened, not hypotheticals.

01

How to read this page

Each item is rated by how likely it is to cause a problem during a demo or real use, not by how severe it would be in theory. Most were found through testing with real forms, not identified by inspection alone.

02

Risks likely to matter for a live demo

Personal information in a PDF is no longer redacted before the AI call High

Since the pivot to a single multimodal classification call (see the integration doc), the whole PDF is sent to the model, not just extracted text. Redaction (src/lib/redact) only ever pattern-matched personal information in text; it has no way to inspect or redact information rendered inside a PDF's pages before those bytes leave the device. This is a deliberate tradeoff agreed with the mentor: most of these forms are public forms already handled in the cloud, and local extraction proved too unreliable to justify keeping the previous redaction step. It should be stated explicitly at a demo, not left implicit.

Status: disclosed tradeoff, not currently mitigated. If this needs to change, redaction would have to move to a pre-send image-level pass, or be dropped in favour of per-model provider privacy terms.
The best-known model consistently fails on New Zealand government forms High

Local testing found three working models across two forms: gemma3:4b, qwen2.5vl:7b, and gemma4:26b (the mentor's recommendation), all producing correct results. A subsequent 16-form cloud batch found google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it:free succeeding on 6 of 16, with the remainder initially attributed to free-tier congestion under batch load. A follow-up round retried those 10 failures individually, with real gaps between attempts, to test that assumption. Six of the ten failed again, even in isolation, and every one of those six is an official New Zealand government form (Inland Revenue, Internal Affairs, Immigration New Zealand, or Work and Income). Every form that succeeded once retried alone was not a government form. A 6-for-6 versus 3-for-3 split is a strong enough signal to treat as a genuine pattern rather than coincidence, though the exact cause (length, layout density, or something specific to how these particular PDFs are constructed) has not been confirmed.

Status: partially resolved. A paid model (google/gemini-2.5-flash) was tested directly against the worst-affected form, a 26-page NZ citizenship application that had timed out three times on the free tier, and succeeded in 5.3 seconds. This is strong evidence the failure was free-tier capacity or rate-limiting rather than a genuine limit on model capability. Paying for a model is a confirmed fix for this issue where a demo requires a long, dense, or government form. See the entry below on paid-model accuracy tradeoffs before selecting a paid model, since not every paid option is a clean upgrade.
Not every paid model is a clean upgrade over the free one Medium

A head-to-head comparison benchmarked the free model against 7 paid candidates on the same 5 real forms. Every paid model was 15 to 35 times faster for 1 to 4 cents per form, a clear win. But two real accuracy problems appeared only in certain paid models, never in the free model, across the same forms. Three models (both Gemini variants, and Qwen2.5-VL) returned the exact same confidence score for every field on at least one form, sometimes every form, which silently defeats the low-confidence flagging this product relies on to protect a blind or low-vision user from a wrong guess. Separately, gpt-4o-mini silently dropped 16 of 23 real questions on one form with no error, and gemini-2.5-flash-lite repeated a structural bug seen earlier, splitting one yes/no question into two disconnected fields.

Recommendation: three models came back clean on every form tested: anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5, google/gemma-3-27b-it (the mentor's original recommendation), and mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct. Of these, the latter two are meaningfully cheaper per form. A paid model should not be selected on speed or price alone; the cheapest and fastest options in this comparison were also the ones with real accuracy problems.
OpenRouter's own PDF-reading step can go down independently of any model Medium

During the 16-form cloud batch, one form failed identically and instantly on all three models tested, with a "Failed to parse" error. The error detail showed OpenRouter uses a separate, shared document-reading service (from Mistral) to convert a PDF into readable text before any model sees it, and that service was briefly unavailable. Retrying the same form alone succeeded cleanly, confirming this was a transient outage rather than a problem with the file.

Status: fixed. src/lib/openrouter/client.ts now retries once, after a short delay, specifically when the error message indicates a parsing failure, the same signature confirmed transient here. A genuinely malformed request (wrong model name, broken payload) is not matched by this and still fails immediately, since retrying would not help in that case.
A network-level timeout gap could silently cut off a slow-but-working request Fixed

A retry round surfaced a bare "fetch failed" on one form after 76 seconds, well under the app's own intended 2-minute limit. The root cause: Node's built-in fetch is built on undici, whose default network agent has its own separate 300-second header timeout that can behave unpredictably under real network conditions, independent of any timeout this app sets deliberately. The same underlying issue was previously found and fixed in the local model-testing tooling.

Status: fixed in src/lib/openrouter/client.ts, the production code every model call goes through, by using undici's fetch directly with an explicit, generous timeout agent, removing the conflict between two different timeout systems.
A malformed model response crashed classification instead of failing gracefully Fixed

Confirmed live on a real fillable form with a real 16-field AcroForm structure: the model occasionally returns a genuinely empty response body with no sections key at all, which crashed with a raw error instead of the clear "we couldn't read that file" message every other classification failure already shows. Before this was understood, the symptom looked like a field write-back problem, fields showing generic placeholder labels or landing blank, but the field write-back logic itself was already correct throughout; it writes each answer into the exact real PDF field its anchor names.

Status: fixed in src/lib/openrouter/classify-pdf.ts, which now checks the response actually contains a sections array before proceeding, and fails with a clear error otherwise. Re-verified directly against the same 16-field form afterward: every detected field mapped correctly onto its real field name.
Scanned photos and image-only forms aren't supported Fixed

PNG and JPG uploads now go through the same classification pipeline as a flat PDF: the whole image is sent to the model in one call, with no AcroForm fields to enumerate since none exist for a photo. Confirming produces a plain question-and-answer summary document, the same fallback already used for a PDF with no fillable fields, since there is no real form structure to write values back into. Only formats outside PDF, PNG, and JPG still show the plain "can't read this yet" message.

03

Risks that were found and already fixed

Listed for transparency. These occurred during development and are resolved, not currently live issues.

A model wrapping its answer in markdown could crash reading the form entirely Fixed

Every AI prompt in this app explicitly instructs the model not to use markdown fencing, but not every model follows that instruction. Found directly while comparing 7 paid models: anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5 wrapped its answer in a code block on every one of 5 real test forms, despite being told not to. The code that reads the model's answer had no defence against this; it would have failed outright with a technical error instead of showing a form, the first time any model decided to add formatting. Fixed by having the app strip that wrapping automatically before reading the answer, regardless of which model sends it.

The live site was quietly using an untested, paid model instead of the free one that was actually tested Fixed

Extensive real-form testing validated google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it:free, but the production code's model setting was never updated to match; it was still pointed at the mentor's original suggestion, google/gemma-3-27b-it, which had never been tested against a real form and turned out to be frequently rate-limited. Found by reproducing a real "couldn't read that file" report and adding proper error logging, since the underlying error had previously been silently discarded; the logs showed the incorrect model name immediately. This was not only a quality issue: it was quietly spending real OpenRouter credits on failed requests instead of using the free, validated model. Fixed by correcting the model setting and redeploying, confirmed with a successful real upload afterward.

Clicking Previous discarded what was typed and didn't show answers already given Fixed

Two related bugs on the question-answering page: clicking Previous, or pressing the P key, before submitting an answer silently discarded whatever had just been typed, and returning to a question that already had an answer showed an empty box instead of what was actually saved. Both traced to the same cause: the on-screen answer box was not actually connected to the saved answers, only to its own temporary memory that was cleared on every question change. Fixed so Previous now saves a genuinely new answer before navigating back, without forcing an answer on a question being deliberately left blank, and revisiting any question always shows what was actually saved.

Confirming with unanswered required questions didn't take you back to them Fixed

Attempting to finish with a required question still unanswered is meant to return the user to it directly. The underlying logic already identified which question that was, but nothing acted on it, so the review page showed no visible change. Fixed so it now genuinely navigates to that question.

A required signature question could not be completed Fixed

Signature questions were presented as an ordinary text box, but any typed input was rejected by design, since this product never accepts a signature typed on a keyboard, only a genuine one signed by hand afterward. The Skip option that should have offered a way past it was hidden whenever the question was required, so a required signature question was a genuine dead end: nothing typed would ever be accepted, and there was no button to proceed. Fixed with a clear explanation and a direct "I'll sign this by hand" action in its place.

A skipped signature question still blocked confirmation forever Fixed

Fixing the dead end above surfaced a second, deeper issue: the review gate treated a skipped signature question exactly like an unanswered one, so confirming was refused and the user was sent back to the same signature question indefinitely, with no way to ever finish a form that required one. A skipped signature is the correct, expected outcome for that question type, not an outstanding answer, so it no longer blocks confirmation.

A single yes-or-no question could be split into two dead-end questions Fixed

On at least one real form, the classification model represented a single yes-or-no question as two separate choice questions instead of one, each offering only "Yes" or only "No" as its sole option. Answering one left the other still outstanding with no way to answer it meaningfully. This kind of split is now detected and merged back into a single question after classification, independent of which model produced it.

Finishing the last question could leave the answer flow on a blank screen Fixed

Answering or skipping the final question moves the underlying engine into its review phase, but the answer flow page had no view for that phase and misread it as an empty form, showing a bare fallback message instead of continuing to the review page. It now recognizes this phase correctly and continues automatically.

Answers from a previous upload could persist into a new one Fixed

Question identifiers are regenerated fresh on every classification call, not held stable across uploads. Starting a new upload without clearing the previous session's answers meant a new form's third question, for example, could silently inherit whatever answer had been given to a completely unrelated previous form's third question. A new upload now clears any answers left over from a prior session first.

"Read back" on the review page didn't actually mark an answer as heard Fixed

The review gate, the rule that every answer must genuinely be heard before finishing, relies on tracking which answers were actually stepped through. Clicking an individual answer's "Read back" button used a different, unrelated action that never updated that tracking. The click appeared to register but never counted, which could make the gate feel unresponsive. Additionally, an unanswered question showed a "Read back" button that made no sense on an empty field; it now correctly reads "Answer this" and navigates to that question.

A page could freeze or crash on certain forms Fixed

Uploading a real large form once caused the review page to crash with a "maximum update depth exceeded" error, a technical issue where the page repeatedly re-checked the same data in a loop. Found through real testing, root-caused, and fixed the same day, then verified with a full retest.

Setting the AI service key on the live site broke every request Fixed

A hidden character was included by accident when saving the AI service's access key to the hosting platform, causing every request to fail with a confusing error. Traced to the exact cause and fixed by re-saving the key a different way.

Personal information wasn't being stripped before the AI call Fixed, then superseded (see the high-severity entry above)

Text extracted from flat forms was sent to the classification model as-is, with no check for names, emails, national ID numbers, or similar personal details it might contain. Fixed by porting the same detection logic the team's shared codebase already used, and confirmed by inspecting the exact text sent to the model during testing: real names and email addresses were replaced with placeholders before leaving the server. This fix no longer applies. The pivot to sending the whole PDF to the model instead of extracted text means the redaction step can no longer inspect the content sent. See the high-severity entry above.

Checkboxes on real PDFs showed as a text box, and field labels were meaningless Fixed

Testing against a real government form with real fillable fields surfaced two separate bugs: checkboxes and multiple-choice fields rendered as a free-text box instead of a selectable control, and on a form whose fields were auto-named by the tool that produced it (labels such as "001", "002"), those meaningless internal names were shown as the question itself. Fixed by rendering the correct control per field type, and by locating the actual label text printed next to each field on the page, a local position-based lookup with no AI model involved, instead of relying on the field's internal name.

04

Lower-urgency items to keep in mind

Filled-document output only covers two cases Known gap

Confirming and finishing produces a real download: a genuinely filled PDF for forms with real fillable fields, or a plain question-and-answer summary for forms read as flat text. There is no intermediate case yet, for example drawing answers onto a flat PDF at their original position, so a flat form's output is a summary document rather than a marked-up copy of the original.

Not yet reconciled with the team's shared codebase Known gap

This branch was built independently to move quickly during the hackathon. Four of the shared codebase's engine packages (form-model, conversation, validate, redact) have since been ported over with unmodified logic, so behaviour matches in those areas, but this remains a hand-copy rather than a real workspace import. A genuine merge is intentionally scheduled for after the next team sync, not something skipped by accident.

Large forms with repeated grids surface every cell as its own question Known gap

A form built as a table, such as a per-room property condition checklist or a per-person schedule, has no concept of "this is one row" in the current field model; every individual box appears as a separate question in the flow. This applies to genuinely large government or legal forms, and no grouping approach has been built for it yet.

No accounts, no saved history between sessions By design, for now

Closing the browser tab clears everything. This matches the project's privacy-first goal of storing nothing on a server, but means there is no way to resume a form later. This is a product decision worth revisiting, not a bug.
